THE LIGHTHOUSE KEEPER

CHAPTER I.

THE VILLAGE OF HARROW POINT HAD NEVER BEEN LARGE, AND IN THE WINTER, WHEN THE FISHING BOATS WERE DRAWN UP ON THE SHINGLE AND THE SUMMER VISITORS HAD LONG SINCE GONE BACK TO THE TOWNS, IT SEEMED SMALLER STILL. A SINGLE ROAD RAN DOWN FROM THE MOOR TO THE HARBOUR, PASSING THE CHURCH, THE INN, AND A ROW OF GREY COTTAGES WHOSE WINDOWS LOOKED OUT TOWARDS THE SEA.

AT THE END OF THE BREAKWATER STOOD THE LIGHTHOUSE. IT WAS NOT A TALL BUILDING, NOR A HANDSOME ONE, BUT FOR NEARLY A HUNDRED YEARS ITS LAMP HAD BEEN LIT EVERY EVENING AT DUSK, AND THE MEN WHO KEPT IT HAD BEEN COUNTED AMONG THE MOST RESPECTED PEOPLE IN THE PARISH. OLD THOMAS WREN HAD KEPT IT FOR THIRTY OF THOSE YEARS, AND HIS FATHER BEFORE HIM FOR TWENTY MORE.

IT WAS A COLD MORNING IN THE FIRST WEEK OF DECEMBER WHEN THE LETTER ARRIVED. THOMAS WAS MENDING A NET ON THE BENCH OUTSIDE HIS DOOR, AS HE DID MOST MORNINGS WHEN THE WEATHER ALLOWED, AND HE DID NOT LOOK UP UNTIL THE POSTMAN HAD CALLED HIS NAME TWICE. THE ENVELOPE WAS THICK AND CREAM-COLOURED, AND IT BORE THE CREST OF THE HARBOUR BOARD IN THE CORNER.

"YOU HAD BETTER READ IT," SAID THE POSTMAN, WHO WAS ALSO HIS NEPHEW AND HAD NEVER IN HIS LIFE BEEN ABLE TO KEEP A SECRET. "THEY SAY IN THE TOWN THAT THE BOARD MEANS TO CHANGE EVERYTHING, AND THAT THERE WILL BE AN ENGINEER SENT DOWN FROM THE CITY BEFORE THE SPRING."

THOMAS TURNED THE ENVELOPE OVER IN HIS HANDS FOR A LONG TIME WITHOUT OPENING IT. HE KNEW WELL ENOUGH WHAT IT WOULD SAY; THERE HAD BEEN TALK OF AN AUTOMATIC LAMP FOR YEARS, EVER SINCE THE NEW LIGHT HAD BEEN PUT UP AT CARRICK HEAD, AND HE HAD ALWAYS SUPPOSED THAT ONE DAY THE TALK WOULD TURN INTO SOMETHING MORE THAN TALK.

CHAPTER II.

THE ENGINEER CAME ON THE FIRST DAY OF MARCH, A YOUNG MAN WITH A LEATHER CASE AND A PAIR OF SPECTACLES THAT HE WAS FOREVER TAKING OFF AND POLISHING ON THE END OF HIS SCARF. HE WAS POLITE, AND RATHER SHY, AND HE ASKED A GREAT MANY QUESTIONS ABOUT THE LAMP, THE CLOCKWORK THAT TURNED IT, AND THE HOURS THAT THE KEEPER WAS ACCUSTOMED TO KEEP THROUGH THE LONG NIGHTS OF THE YEAR.

THOMAS ANSWERED EVERY QUESTION AS WELL AS HE COULD, AND WHEN HE COULD NOT ANSWER HE SAID SO PLAINLY. HE SHOWED THE YOUNG MAN THE LOGBOOKS, WHICH WENT BACK TO THE YEAR THE LIGHT WAS FIRST LIT, AND THE BRASS INSTRUMENTS THAT HIS FATHER HAD KEPT POLISHED, AND THE LITTLE ROOM AT THE TOP OF THE STAIR WHERE THE KEEPER SAT THROUGH THE NIGHT WITH HIS PIPE AND HIS KETTLE.

ON THE THIRD EVENING THE ENGINEER STAYED TO WATCH THE LAMP BEING LIT. HE STOOD AT THE WINDOW WHILE THE SUN WENT DOWN BEHIND THE HEADLAND, AND HE SAID NOTHING AT ALL UNTIL THE GREAT LENS HAD BEGUN TO TURN AND THE FIRST LONG BEAM HAD SWEPT OUT ACROSS THE WATER TOWARDS THE ROCKS WHERE THE OLD WRECK LAY.
